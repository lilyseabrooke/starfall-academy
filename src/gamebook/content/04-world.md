Starfall Academy is a system formed and defined by its setting, and in this section, you’ll find the juicy details of what that setting actually looks like, to help you get into the setting and either play or run a game more authentic to the Starfall Academy universe.

The system is set in the modern-day real world, just, improved with magic. You’ll find most of the world is similar to our own, and real-world locations aren’t too dissimilar to those in our regular life.

Magic isn’t a secret or anything like that—for better or worse, everyone knows magic exists. It’s almost mundane, in a way: magic academies are public institutions that have to handle paperwork to receive their national and federal grants to continue operating, and congressional chambers the world over argue over appropriate funding bills for their magic academies, with some politicians dismissing the whole field as “impractical wand-waving.” They’re typically nonmagical and are probably just jealous.

However, magical aptitude is rare enough, and often linked with wealth and privilege because of the benefits it confers, that the vast majority of people see magic as something they would never have a chance at, something that will never be relevant to their lives unless they need to go to a specialist hospital and receive restoration magic alongside modern medicine.

Some people yearn for it, eating up every episode of the latest corny TV drama about arcanists and reading romance novels about a person who gets marriage-of-convenienced into a powerful magic family. Some people despise magic as a symbol of the elite, clueless out-of-touch scholars who will never understand real hardship, and write hate comments on social media, guest columns in newspapers opining that the arcane arts should be abolished, and lead protests at magic academy recruitment centers.

But most people just acknowledge it as a thing for other people and go on with their lives.

Some people, however, have the gift—or earn it through blood, sweat and tears.

(Usually it’s the former. It’s kind of unfair, but it is what it is.)

## Magic

:::quote
***Magic*** ***is alive,*** *and it has its will—its chaotic, dangerous will that has claimed many a spellcaster too ambitious for their own good. But like anything else that lives, it too can be tamed.*   - **Professor Moira Finnegan, evoker and summoner,***currently recovering from an exter attack that she thought she could tame*
:::

Magic is the key element at the center of Starfall’s existence, a form of using one’s vital soul essence to reshape reality.

**Ley lines** are the root of magic in the world, lines of pure magic energy that run through the earth itself. These **ley lines** are thin and weak in much of the world, but they concentrate into wellsprings of magical energy called **ley line outcroppings,** around which magic tends to be chaotic and destructive.

The world’s highest concentration of ley line outcroppings lies directly underneath the Starfall campus—what led the Academy’s five Founders to lay its foundations there. Although magic is studied in many ley-line heavy areas around the world, the ley lines of Starfall Academy have forever cemented its place as the forerunner in the magical world, and “Starfall” itself is often used as a synecdoche for magic users and scholars of the arcane around the world.

Much to the dismay of all the arcanists who aren’t from Starfall.

:::quote
**CHARACTER PORTRAIT****Rowena Blackcliff (she/they)** *An architect of modern geomancy, the study of ley lines, an ancient field of magic that was long considered discredited. Rowena’s control of ley line magics was instrumental in protecting Starfall Academy during the Basilisk Incident.**They grew up without a family to speak of, claimed in the same ley magic incident that gave Rowena her connection to magic, and made themself useful to a clan of vagrants and drifters with her ley line meditation technique.**After her dramatic involvement in the Basilisk Incident, Rowena has devoted herself to redeveloping the field of geomancy, now a traveling scholar and guest lecturer at magic academies the world over, helping raise the next generation of geomancers.**That said, they’re still a bit broody. People are afraid to praise their work for fear of receiving a terrible curse on their bloodline or something. Maybe it’s RBF.*
:::

Magical aptitude in people is rare—although anybody can cast magic, and anybody can study enough to learn how to develop magical skill, what takes years and years of steady practice to achieve might be effortless to somebody born to incredible magic power, an aptitude often passed down genetically.

Magic aptitude doesn’t always come from genetics, though, and many people have more complex stories. Fey and demons are often known to barter with humans who have little to no magical aptitude, offering promises of magic power in exchange for something inscrutable. Sometimes people take matters into their own hands, whether with intensive work and study to improve their natural magical aptitude or with questionable magic experiments to force it to happen.

Other times, the ley lines simply choose somebody to have magical aptitude. While miraculous, the ley lines are also said to be the architects of fate itself, and if a child is born with magical aptitude for no apparent reason, it’s said to be a sign fate has much in store for them.

It also gets people accused of having affairs. Or sometimes it’s an excuse used to cover up an affair with a sexy arcanist passing through town.

## Starfall Academy

:::quote
***Starfall Academy is open to all****. Just don’t open every door you see.*   - **common refrain from the official Starfall Academy Welcoming Guide**
:::

The dramatic spires of Starfall Citadel, clock tower cutting its shape out against starlit skies, its Houses bustling with students each brimming with their own ambition, ley lines powering the mysterious swirling magics of Glimmerdeep Lake and the ever-present dangers of Amber Woods—Starfall Academy is the backdrop for most games in the system, unsurprisingly.

One of the world’s largest magic academies both by size and population, Starfall Academy is a massive, sprawling campus dedicated to the study of the arcane, nestled in northern New York State, just shy of the Canadian border. The academy is large enough, especially in its staggering Starfall Citadel, to count as its own city, with just as many people living and working among the halls and buildings of the Citadel as there are students in the academy’s five Houses, each House representing one of the academy’s illustrious five founders.

There’s space in Starfall Academy for all types of games, so don’t feel limited by what other stories may already exist within the confines of Starfall. You can play the system anywhere in the world, really—maybe the ancient, esteemed legacies of Albion Academy of the Arcane Arts in the United Kingdom, or maybe among the thrumming activity and sky-high ambition of the students of Baranomichi Colégio in Brazil—but most games fall back to the familiar halls of Starfall Academy and its Houses, and below, you’ll find an extensive description of important elements of Starfall Academy.

:::quote
**CHARACTER PORTRAIT****Valentine-Emma Whisperkeep (she/her)** *The current head of the Whisperkeep family, a magic family specializing in Divination, dating back centuries with a legacy that makes it practically royalty—and which enjoys, comfortably, the status of Starfall Academy’s largest donor.**With Valentine-Emma being particularly young when she took over the role of family head, questions circled about whether she would uphold the legacy of the Whisperkeep name being on countless buildings through Starfall Academy. However, in terms of public relations, little has changed under her leadership.**Inside sources report it’s because she wants to name everything after her favorite candies, and she has reliable aides who politely and respectfully talk her out of it. We can neither confirm nor deny these reports.*
:::

### Starfall Citadel

Starfall Academy is built around the illustrious **Starfall Citadel** at the center, old construction that goes back over 200 years and has been continuously built and rebuilt and built upon, resulting in a massive castle-like sprawl where sometimes old chambers have been simply built up around and forgotten below it all.

A large share of the Academy’s buildings are here in the **Citadel,** which is split up into many small **districts** each with their own character.

The often haphazard city planning of the **Citadel** leads to the perfect hiding places for all kinds of mysteries—forgotten doors, narrow back alleys, underground plazas that once held magic shops and now hold a demon’s secret lair of minor creatures, and of course, places that could be the perfect restaurant for a date. We recommend Il Maiale. It’s an Italian place with some great tiramisù.

:::quote
***Count the stairs.*** *Go wherever you like in the Citadel, but count the stairs. If you’re ever more than three flights down from where you went in… ask yourself if you really need to be there.*   - **Nadine Nasrallah, Enforcer Agent with the Beacon Commission, tasked with***finding lost students, in a quote that’s trying to give herself less business? weird*
:::

The majority of classes are held in class halls somewhere in the **Citadel**, which means your character will likely be visiting the **Citadel** at least for part of their class period. You probably have a path that you’re sure is the fastest way from one class hall to the next, and you’re willing to argue with others on what the fastest way is. Maybe your fastest way is teleportation. You couldn’t be faulted.

Aside from just having class halls, the **Citadel** is where many people who aren’t students but are connected to the arcane world live and work. Many faculty members have homes in the **Citadel,** whether luxurious townhouses on beautiful side streets in **Brownbark** or apartments above magic shops lining the streets in **Stormbringer Way,** or a seedy basement spot in **Aelin Hallows** that’s probably not legally licensed as a residence but the rent was really low, so what can you do?

The **Citadel** is packed full of shops, services, museums, cafes, wand studios, gardens, restaurants, and of course, importantly, bubble tea shops. Anything your character might want is probably somewhere in the **Citadel,** maybe in a beautiful glittery building at the top of a white-brick street in the **Astral Quarter**, or maybe sold out of a window in **Mercy Square** where they have to check a divination rune first to make sure you’re not an Enforcer.

The **Citadel** is the perfect place to go looking for secrets. The problem is just that you usually find them.

### The Grounds

The more wide-open, nature-rich parts of campus outside the Citadel are referred to by many names, but the **Grounds** is the most common name for everything that lies beyond the Citadel’s spires.

The **Grounds** are wide and varied, as the unpredictable magic of the ley lines helped carve dramatic variations in terrain and biomes in the area of the academy, and with the exception of **Scorpion House,** all the Academy’s **Houses** are spread throughout the **Grounds.**

Paths in the **Grounds** wind through idyllic fields, along steep cliffs, through manicured gardens and wild woods, past the shimmering blues of lakes and ponds, and tangle with many small outcroppings of buildings, whether class halls, homes, magic laboratories, greenhouses, shady businesses, or even not-so-shady businesses.

For those who want to live and work at Starfall without squeezing into the Citadel, homes nestled out among the wild landscapes of the **Grounds** are the best option, so long as you don’t mind a commute where you might encounter scaleworm swarms, griffin battles, or worse still, a student trying a cool new trick.

Although most classes are held in the Citadel, a good quarter or so of classes are held in places out in the **Grounds** instead, especially for herbalists, bestiologists, and draconologists.

It’s very easy to wander off into wild areas of the **Grounds** and become very lost, very quickly, especially if you accidentally stroll into a fey enclave or find a magic grotto that puts you out in a different place than where you entered, but generally, the **Grounds** are safe. Generally.

:::quote
**CHARACTER PORTRAIT****Emmett Stone (he/him)** *The beast warder credited primarily for the creation of the* ***Wards*** *that protect campus, although the system was too much of a team effort to truly attribute to one person. Emmett Stone was a renowned bestiologist who made significant strides in protective methods very advanced for his time almost a hundred years ago. He left the world a proud and beloved champion of the people of Starfall.**Now his descendents make boatloads of money illegally trafficking beasts. It happens.*
:::

The outermost boundary of the **Grounds** is marked by the **Starfall Academy superplanar presence-inverse warding system,** typically referred to as just the **Wards.** This is a bubble of protective warding magic placed around the entirety of campus—most of the boundary itself is deep in woods or far-flung cliff territory, and it’s rare for a student to actually see the **Wards**—that protects it from the magic creatures that naturally pack in around Starfall’s rich ley line presence.

The **Wards** are a marvel of arcane engineering that turn creatures’ own magic power against them, providing more shielding force the more powerful a creature is, allowing harmless biodiversity to populate the rich natural areas of Starfall while keeping out dangerous predators that would do unwanted things like eat students. (Early Starfall administrators found out that’s bad PR.)

However, as brilliant as the system is, no system is perfect. Through bartering and clever maneuvering, some more powerful creatures find workarounds to enter the **Wards,** but those clever enough to find a way through are also clever enough to keep their heads down and try not to get noticed, which means they generally don’t go eating students.

The **Wards** are also graded, meaning that some stronger creatures can pass through the early parts of the **Wards**, while other areas are stricter. This is a measure to maintain healthy biodiversity, and it also means that some areas in the **Grounds**—referred to as **wild areas**—will have more significant magical creatures in them, while still keeping out things like stone giants that want to kill all arcanists.

You can also go beyond the **Wards** if you want—they don’t block humans. But keep in mind that is a bad idea.

The most important parts of the **Grounds** are the locations of the four Houses outside the Citadel: **Jewelstone Hollow, Ryker Cliffs, Glimmerdeep Lake,** and the **Amber Woods.**

#### Jewelstone Hollow

:::quote
***Jewelstone Hollow,*** *beautiful place to explore. Make sure you see the old steelnose tunnels. And whatever you do, if something talks to you and you don’t know what it is—don’t listen.*   - **Baya Arclight, FDM Agent and Dragon House graduate**
:::

The home of **Dragon House,** **Jewelstone Hollow** lies due north of the Citadel.

**Jewelstone Hollow** is a dry, rocky region with plenty of caves rich with magic and draconic creatures, and is especially known for its connection to crystallomantic magics and necromancy. The magic of the **Hollow** comes alive at night, especially, as luminis—small, luminescent birds—are especially fond of nesting in the thick, pine-like magic trees that grow in the rich magic soil here, which means their lights glow in the trees at nighttime.

It’s a macabre kind of beauty, and not just because of Dragon House’s dark Gothic spires, but because cemeteries are also common here—not just human ones, either. Draconic creatures, who are often intelligent enough to hold their own funerals and burial grounds, are especially drawn to the **Hollow,** which means you may find a dragon graveyard in a mysterious cavern. It’s probably not cursed, but you can never be too sure.

Classes held in this area are likely to be draconic fieldwork, necromancy (especially creature necromancy), or specialized crystallomancy studios.

If your character explores **Jewelstone Hollow,** expect to find underground structures, from caves to tombs, which often have draconic or even demonic connections. Or wine cellars from the eccentric rich people who have big houses in the high hilly areas on the northeast side. The wine cellars are usually the most cursed places of all.

#### Ryker Cliffs

:::quote
***Race you to Glimmerdeep.***   - **every hotshot evoker**
:::

The home of **Eagle House,** the **Ryker Cliffs** make Eagles fall into one of three camps: teleporters, flying evokers, or students with really strong legs.

The furthest house from the Citadel, Eagle House and the **Ryker Cliffs** around it are positioned to the west of campus, dramatic cliffs that swoop upwards into the mountains that lie beyond. The **Ryker Cliffs** vary between dry, barren rock on the south side and swathes of alpine forest on the north side, where the cliffs meet the expanse of the **Amber Woods.**

Despite being a little harsher than the other major areas of the **Grounds,** the **Ryker Cliffs** do offer one thing in spades: stunning views. Not just of campus, but of the sky: there is a long and storied tradition of divination in this area, and even the esteemed Whisperkeep clan—perhaps the world’s most famous (or infamous) family of diviners—has a tradition of sending their children to Eagle House.

The **Ryker Cliffs** make the perfect place for flying. Flying from Eagle House to Glimmerdeep Lake is the definitive flight path, the furthest two points outside the wild areas, and many students push their luck and get hurt trying it. They keep trying it, though.

Classes held in this area are often practical divination classes, bestiology fieldwork, or flight-based evocation.

If your character explores the **Ryker Cliffs,** expect to find dramatic peaks and valleys, with daring cliff faces and plenty of big creatures that live in caves around this area. Water features are also dramatic in the area—expect hidden water caverns, small but intense waterfalls, and rapids gushing through narrow ravines. Expect also to find abandoned workshops—the **Ryker Cliffs** are popular for people who want to be alone and hidden from view of the Academy, making it ideal for rogue artificers, enchanters, and crystallomancers. Worse than the abandoned workshops is finding occupied workshops.

#### Glimmerdeep Lake

:::quote
***You can tell when a potion’s from Glimmerdeep.*** *It just hits different when you know someone risked getting eaten by a giant magic turtle to get it, you know?*   - **Shauna, a nonmagical celebrity who likes to appropriate magic culture**
:::

The home of **Dolphin House,** **Glimmerdeep Lake** is a symbol of peace, tranquility, and love at Starfall, although some people don’t like Dolphins, so for those people, it’s more just a symbol of, like, “ugh, Dolphins.” It lies to the east of campus and is, of all the major regions of the **Grounds,** the safest and most peaceful.

That doesn’t mean it’s safe and peaceful. Just that it’s the safest and most peaceful of all of them.

**Glimmerdeep Lake** is a large, complex water feature fitted in among the low forested hills and the rocky crags of the wild areas beyond, and it shimmers with a rich sapphire blue all year round, owing to the deep magics that run through the lake, rich with the illusory powers that permeate the area. The water has incredible arcane purity, and is the gold standard for alchemy bases.

It’s not small, but it’s still much, much deeper than it looks—diving into the lake would reveal a massive, deep tunnel complex that sprawls outwards into chambers where giant magic creatures live, and some paths of it eventually run to the ocean, facilitating the long journeys of water wyrms.

The areas around **Glimmerdeep Lake** are beautiful and varied, from tiny harbors squeezed in along the banks, to cute brick bridges running over tributaries, to secluded walkways with long rope bridges running past dramatic waterfalls, to enchanted grottos, and of course, the water, which is, you know, full of magic creatures. They like to pop out and have a look around sometimes.

Classes in this area are likely to be illusion, water-based evocation, and alchemy.

If your character explores **Glimmerdeep Lake,** expect to find the most interesting things underwater—caves accessible only by swimming, sunken locales, and underwater caverns that have been used as hiding places for rogue arcanists before. Outside of the water, expect to find people enjoying themselves—it’s popular for picnics. It’s also popular for duels! Duelists love a dramatic backdrop.

#### Amber Woods

:::quote
***Boar House!*** *Oh, I love it. You go in and you make three new friends before they realize you’re not supposed to be there, and they escort you to the door, but those three friends still come to your wedding. To someone you met at Boar House!*   - **Professor Catheryn Gracey, who married a Boar House graduate**
:::

The home of **Boar House,** the **Amber Woods** sprawl over a massive area north of campus, blending up into Ryker Cliffs in the east and extending on for what feels like forever to the north and the west.

Of all the regions of the **Grounds,** the **Amber Woods** are the biggest within the **Wards.** Boar House is set shallowly in at the base, to the northwest of the Citadel, and the **Amber Woods** extend far beyond, rich with unexplored mysteries. Along with many beasts, the fey especially make their home in the **Amber Woods,** and anyone who wanders off into the **Amber Woods** is likely to find the most beautiful magic they’ve ever seen, shortly before they end up in some weird fey trap.

Many incredible, rare plants grow in places deep in the tangled mass of the **Amber Woods,** and many herbalists have gone missing looking for them. That hasn’t stopped new herbalists from doing just that.

Classes in this area are likely to be bestiology and herbalism, with a decent number of wandcrafting studios here too.

If your character explores the **Amber Woods,** expect to find beasts both magic and mundane, as well as rich jungle-like terrain affected by magic—walking trees, massive mushrooms, and carnivorous plants that talk are basically a cliche here. Because of the size, the ease of getting lost, and the draw of ancient magic here, many remnants of lost peoples and cultures are here—you may find ancient buildings, mysterious temples, and the like by exploring far from the beaten path. Sometimes their forgotten magics are still there for the taking.

## Starfall’s Houses

:::quote
***Imperare est vivare.***   - **Dragon House motto**
:::

Students at Starfall Academy join one of five Houses, each corresponding to one of the academy’s five Founders, who are legendary figures among the academy. Each House embodies some of the important virtues of its associated Founder—as well as often being associated with some of the flaws of that Founder as well.

Hey, you win some, you lose some.

Students choose their House when they join Starfall, and they can change their choice of House between semesters, but it’s rare—students often develop an intense loyalty to their chosen House and often only leave if something goes wrong in their House.

And it goes without saying that the Houses develop intense rivalries between one another.

### The Five Houses

For your character, you’ll have to think carefully about which House you want to be in, although most games will work smoothest with everyone in the same House.

Discuss it with your GM and your party and decide which one is most right for you and your character.

:::house-explorer

#### Eagle House

Eagle House is the home of scholars and sharp-eyed experts alike. It might seem like a contrast between the lofty scholarly types in their stone towers at the highest peaks of Starfall Academy and the relentlessly practical spellslingers who are always out among the cliffs or exploring for powerful magic secrets in the woods, but every Eagle is united by this idea to develop great skill.

Skill, craft, and cunning instinct are central to the Eagle’s ethos. Students of this House commit to what they do and reach for quiet excellence, and when you need a difficult job done, look no further than an Eagle.

##### The House

This House is an ancient castle-like building constructed into the jagged peaks of Ryker Cliff, which stand at the west edge of campus, isolating it from the rest of the world. Its stone bridges span the perilous drops of the cliffs, and great stone platforms jut out over infinity.

Some of the House’s construction can be bare and brutal in places, like the distant watchtowers at the far edges of its reach in isolated parts of the cliffs, but the majority of it is rich and beautifully lavish, with great plazas and coliseum-like builds swathed in many plants, but especially the jade-green bushes of hookblossom that were a signature of the House’s Founder, Silence, and that bloom in tiny fire-orange hook-shaped flowers in the spring.

Walking through the intricate, lavish archways and down the promenades on a springtime’s night, lit up with the dancing glows of the hundreds of magic lights that flicker and float like fairies, the hookblossoms brilliant orange and the moon so big and so full that this high up it feels like you can reach up and touch it—great poets and painters have long been inspired by the breathtaking sceneries of Eagle House, and many great works of art trace back to inspiration found in these halls.

The practical layout of the halls show clearly the commitment to skill, craft, and personal greatness. Many of the dorms in this House are in self-contained sections that frequently boast practice ranges, studies, bustling alchemy labs, and enchanters’ workbenches littered in half-finished projects from a dozen people all at once, but also lush living spaces and common areas with garden terraces and small, beautifully-detailed halls that aren’t known to a lot of people outside that particular section, meaning every turn and every crevice of this House is filled with hidden gems.

Because of this layout, many Eagles fall naturally into their own smaller groups with the rest of their section, and Eagles tend to have small groups of people who they trust with their lives, rather than giving shallower trust freely out to many people.

That said, the House has plenty of main areas that it’s known for, and the ones that end up in all the Instagram reels from people visiting Starfall Academy, and the big spots where people take pictures posing in front of the landmarks and send to their friends and family to make them jealous.

##### Points of Interest

The Prince’s Walk is the first thing you find if you enter the House from the front, a beautiful arched hall with intricate, detailed paintings on the walls and the ceiling, lined with precious, ceremonial artifacts placed on display along the sides of the hall. The diamond tile floor in white and hookblossom-orange that spans the Walk is symbolic of Eagle House, and it comes alive with activity regularly for Eagle House events that pack into the halls and fill it with life, laughter, and the kind of boisterous rivalry that springs up between Eagle House students.

The Amber Lady’s Promenade, typically just The Promenade, offers likely the greatest views of Starfall Academy, a lavish outdoor space with arches that run the perimeter lush with hookblossoms, leading out to small rounded platforms like juliette balconies with all of Starfall as a view, overlooking the campus from above. The main space is a broad, open-air plaza with a marble statue in a fountain in the center, a famous statue of an eagle slaying a griffin, representing the Great Eagle’s conquest over the authorities that Starfall Academy was founded in defiance of. Is it a little morbid? Yeah, probably, but it’s all over the internet so everyone’s desensitized to it. The balcony platforms are a favorite of Eagle House students to take off flying from, often to race each other to Starfall Citadel, to loop around the House, or just to take shots at each other, inevitably fall, and wind up with a stern talking-to in the infirmary.

The Waterglow Observatory is the tallest room in the House, standing at a staggering—and probably excessive–eight stories tall. The room is an art installation as much as it is a room, multiple winding staircases joining and splitting from where they start thick and full in the center of the floor, like branches of a tree or a river, winding up to platforms and walkways all the way up to the top, where the observatory platform once let scholars observe the stars high above any distractions, but nowadays it’s mostly just for playing hooky or taking a date if you’re basic. In a sealed display in the center of the top floor is the House Artifact, the Hunter’s Eye, an enchanted bow that once belonged to the Great Eagle.

But of course, the most important place in Eagle House is the place that means the most in your heart. Which is probably the small common kitchens attached to some of the common areas. Eagles are excellent cooks. Usually. Not always.

##### Founder and Culture

The legacy of the House is wrapped up in its founder, Silence, the Great Eagle. A kind of professional mercenary who sold their skills to militias or as protection for important figures, Silence—their real name a hot topic of contention, as they ascribed to “throw away their humanity” and “become only an idea”—was an expert in hunting and killing.

That said, most Eagle House students don’t follow in their footsteps that way. The majority of Eagles have never killed anyone.

Silence studied interplanar magics at Academia de las Maravillas in Spain in the mid-1700s, before the formal field of teleportation as we know it today was created, and they are often credited as one of the main figures responsible for teleportation becoming a full, formalized field of magic as it is today. With tensions brewing around what would become the American Revolutionary War, they found plenty of gainful employment in the New World, and that would lead to them helping found Starfall Academy.

Despite the sordid legacy they had—if you were to bring them up and ask them, the thing they would probably say they were most proud of was the exact number of people they killed—Silence’s image has been softened and cleaned up over the years since their death, and they are now considered a hero of skill, cunning instinct, and intense scholarship.

In this legacy, Eagle House students strive for mastery in their fields above all else. Sometimes this makes them unapproachable, or gives them a cold air like they think they’re better than you, and—well, sometimes they do think that.

But sometimes they just genuinely are better than you. So I guess it’s fair.

##### Playing an Eagle

If you play a character in this House, consider how well they fit in—or don’t. What is the driving intention that pushes them onward? What is this thing they need to be better at, and what will happen if they don’t?

You’ll likely have other people close by in these ornate halls as well. Work with the GM to decide any important relationships you might have with people living in the same section as you within this House. If you and your party are all in Eagle House together, probably at least one of them is in the same section as you, and any who aren’t are likely to be treated with a little less trust.

#### Boar House

Boar House is the ultimate low-drama good vibes House, because your prototypical Boar House student just wants to hang out with their friends, enjoy some food and drink, laugh and share stories, and occasionally remember to do their classwork.

Students from this House come in all varieties, but they all share the same sense of camaraderie, mutual deep trust, and enjoying life with the people they care about. The House where found families come together and people stay up late drinking in the halls and sharing stories that you’re pretty sure are made up, Boars might seem a dysfunctional group at times, but the bonds they have with one another are incomparable. When they band together, it’s a sight to behold.

They’re just usually banding together with the cause of getting a really big dinner on or something. But hey. They’re having fun.

##### The House

This House carries more of a rustic, cozy feel than the others, more of a sense of home and community. Set just deep enough into the lush, magically charged vegetation of the Amber Woods that enclose campus from the north for it to feel like a world apart, the House isn’t tall and grand, but is spread out as a sprawling complex that naturally weaves into the woods, like its Tudor-style latticed facades and wooden columns grow up from the earth.

It has a wider footprint than any of the other Houses, sprawling out deeper into the woods with winding halls and covered wooden bridges that cross the little rambling streams and brooks of Amber Woods, with more gardens than you could count and fenced-in verandas stocked full of garden beds that are absolutely bursting with plants. Courtyards big and small pepper the layout between buildings, and secret nooks out little back doors, overlooking quiet ponds and rich wild zones, are so plentiful that everyone knows one or two they’re pretty sure nobody else does.

The interiors live up to the mood, too, with heavy emphasis on sustainable wood construction and beautiful interlocking wood designs, and even the grandest of grand halls inside have the feel of a warm, welcoming tavern where people share stories by the big, bold fireplaces that are so common in Boar House.

It perhaps isn’t surprising that there’s so many spaces to eat and drink in this House. The only thing Boars really need aside from food and drink is someone to share it with. Music constantly fills the halls, good and bad music alike—nothing unites Boars faster than song.

In short, a cottagecore influencer could make a lifetime’s worth of content in this place.

##### Points of Interest

The House is just as much outdoor zones as it is house, and students here quickly get comfortable with the outdoors if they aren’t already. Many of its paths wind through wild zones alongside brooks and over meandering trails along mossy boulders and over the roots of great trees. The wards up around the main campus areas keep anything too dangerous from roaming these woods, but plenty of animals—and plenty of fey—populate the areas around Boar House. Waking up to a galobalo in your room is as common as anything.

The most famous and recognizable feature of Boar House is the Champion’s Garden, a beautiful courtyard in the center of the main hall at the front. Enclosed all around by the forest-green and burnt-red facades of the building and interlocking wood lattices, the Champion’s Garden is a massive space with common areas inside charming wooden fences with magic lanterns on them and small garden beds full of fairy bushes that flower year-round and elm trees that are considered good luck and people tie their names to little wooden plaques and hang them from their boughs to bring good luck for upcoming exams, performances, or maybe just asking a cutie out. At the center, of course, is the only thing that could be at the center: a massive statue of the Great Boar, Reginaeus, carrying the House Artifact, an enormous enchanted sword called the Skysplitter. Despite some incidents, the Skysplitter remains relatively unguarded, far and away the easiest House Artifact to steal, which is probably why it gets stolen so often. The Great Boar loves an epic yarn, though, so he’d probably want his sword to continue going on dramatic adventures like that.

Every Wednesday evening, the Hall of the Saints earns its status as the most exciting place in Boar House, and another key symbol of the House. The largest single space in the House, the room is a massive two-story construction that evokes the feeling of being deep in the forest—as if you couldn’t just walk outside for it. Great trees grow up through earthen plots worked into the floor, and thick rows of planters hang from the wooden catwalks that weave like rope bridges between trees among the second level, up to the Calswick Rotunda at the center, a wooden platform that spans around an ancient magic tree called Zeitfliegen, said to be over two thousand years old and transplanted here to keep it alive after a ley line incident nearly killed it, the Hall built around it. It’s up on the Rotunda that the House administrators put on speeches and important announcements every Wednesday to where everyone is gathered through the Hall, and it lights up afterwards into a party like they’ll never party again, as if it’s not going to happen again next Wednesday, while performers take the stage on the Rotunda. Many great music acts have their origins on that stage. Many bad attempts at music acts have their origins on that stage, too.

The Temple of Blissful Dreams is one of the most widely-photographed spots in Boar House as well, a beautiful ornate building at the top of a hill dense with the rare magic growth of passionoak trees and their brilliant crimson bark. There are six hundred and fourteen steps made of red passionoak wood that lead up to the temple, winding up through the foliage and around the tiny waterfalls that trickle down slick stones on the way to the bottom, the six hundred fourteen steps representing the six hundred and fourteen nightmares that the great prophet of hypnomancy Isabella Reyes had to bear before she uncovered the secrets that led to the formalization of hypnomancy as a magic field. The temple itself is a beautifully designed construction in the characteristic forest-green and burnt-red that makes up so much of Boar House, and is ornately built to augment the flow of the ley lines coming through, which leads both to many students performing quiet magic meditation there to hone their power and, also, to many magic beasts and fey flocking around the place. It’s not uncommon to get your ear plucked by a pixie on your way up. Carry orange juice—fey hate oranges.

##### Founder and Culture

The Founder of Boar House, Reginaeus, was a man who left an explosive legacy, and who never met a problem brute force couldn’t solve. His legacy of Creation Magics lives on in Boar House students—many alchemists, enchanters, runologists, herbalists, and wandcrafters trace their origins back to studies in Boar House.

Reginaeus and the Great Dragon, V, were the first of the Founders to meet, although not on friendly terms—Reginaeus, a young boy near Boston in the mid-1700s with a much less powerful name, was an apprentice in carpentry when one of his wild streaks led him to dragon’s territory and an encounter that took his arm and nearly his life, but left him marked with powerful magic. Without much to lose as a carpentry apprentice with only one arm, he took up increasingly powerful and risky experiments with his newfound magic power, and ended up creating some of the earliest magic weapons as he pioneered the field of enchantment as we know it nearly by himself. By the time he met the Great Dragon again ten years later, he was a magic weaponsmith who was contributing greatly to the anti-colonialist organizing against the British, not out of any sense of patriotism, but for a burning desire to see what his weapons could do, now with an enchanted mechanical arm to replace the first one the Great Dragon took.

The story of how an encounter like theirs led to a close friendship and ultimately the founding of Starfall Academy is a tale often told in Boar House, and there is nothing considered more classically Boar House than befriending your enemies and striking down your mutual enemies together.

Reginaeus went on to create ever greater and more powerful magic tools and weapons that would shape much of history, and with his wild and contrary streaks never going anywhere, the decision to found Starfall Academy in defiance of authority was as natural as anything.

##### Playing a Boar

Reginaeus’s drive for honor and glory carries through to the great storytelling tradition of Boar House students today, who are prone to doing anything if they think they can get a good tale out of it. When you play a Boar House student, consider how you engage with the culture of thrilling tales and searching for glory, or if you’re just in Boar House because you like a more laid-back attitude. Either way, you are likely charmingly uncomplicated and easy to get along with because of it, and you likely have your circle of close friends that may be truly massive.

But hey. As long as you get to get a big dinner together, right?

#### Scorpion House

The newest House in the Academy, Scorpion House is still struggling to find its identity, only founded in recent years after the long-buried identity of the fifth Founder, Ariin, was uncovered. Although it’s found some struggles on its path to be taken as seriously as its four big siblings, slowly but steadily it’s found its footing as an elegant house with a strong focus on the arts, collaboration, and community.

Scorpions are the students most likely to lend a helping hand, the peacekeepers, and the ones who make a group work, bringing out the best of everyone around them. These students tend to see the best in everybody and believe in goodness, which leads to many, many beautiful things and also to them kind of being doormats or really easily tricked.

Of course, that’s not to say all Scorpions are useful fools. Just as Ariin was said to be the most influential of the five founders because she was the only one who could get the other four to cooperate, successful Scorpions know that the beating heart of a group or community is also the place with the most power and influence.

And also like their founder, Scorpions might be a little… vain. But there’s nothing wrong with that.

##### The House

Scorpion House is set squarely in the middle of the hustle and bustle of Starfall Citadel, more of a metropolitan feel to its environs, situated just in front of the Cloudless Tower that serves as one of the key symbols of Starfall Academy.

With a beautiful, detailed Art Nouveau style, the House is elegant in the smallest aspects of its designs, from the stained-glass domed ceiling of the Bellblossom Hall to the sweeping archways of the Emberlight Gardens, to every curved, sweeping staircase and every little balcony with intricate wrought-iron rails, every inch of it screams art, beauty, and an obsession with pretty things. I mean, can you blame them?

Students’ dorms are gathered into small self-contained groupings called galerias, with anywhere from five to twenty students’ dorms and common areas that include studios, studies, kitchens, and of course, cozy but beautiful dining rooms to gather and talk magic, art, politics, the weather—Scorpions practically revere the fine art of conversation and can make anything into a thrilling topic to debate for the fun of it. Unlike the sections in Eagle House, for example, Scorpions choose their galeria and will often gather together the people they like the most into their own, so every galeria tends to end up like a little chosen family, although they can be just as dysfunctional as any family.

##### Playing a Scorpion

Scorpions, at their heart, love people. This might take different forms: maybe they love gathering together communities, maybe they love being the center of attention, maybe they have a crush on everyone they meet, or maybe they want to create an army of lackeys to crush the world, but regardless of how it looks, you as a Scorpion are defined by how you engage with people.

You can play a misanthrope in this House if you so choose, but consider how your path led you here. Maybe you want deep down to be proven wrong, maybe you want to learn how people work so you can control them, or maybe you’re just following somebody else’s footsteps or coming here to make a point, but either way, it’s an unusual circumstance, and being around Scorpions is likely to change how you feel over time.

Most likely, your relationships are the most important things to you. Consider what that looks like for you, and how it affects your character’s goals and motivations. Are you engaging with people out of genuine love and affection, or are you just looking for outside validation?

The arts are deeply important to most Scorpions as well, in many forms—music, dance, writing, painting, or a hundred others. Consider what role art plays in your life, or if it doesn’t, how it feels to be a more down-to-earth type in a galeria around a bunch of weird artists.

At the end of the day, though, you still love nothing more than to reunite with them all over dinner for a good-natured argument.

#### Dragon House

:::quote
***Here, you feel the beating heart of Starfall.*** *The Great Dragon was the heart of the founding. And Dragon House has never forgotten.*   - **Head Enforcer Florence Walker**
:::

Set in the obsidian ridges of Jewelstone Hollow, Dragon House stands imposing over all those who approach, tall Gothic spires in black stone conveying the central message of Dragon House’s ethos: to inspire awe without so much as saying a word.

Dragons tend to say a lot of words, though.

Dragon House is the house of leaders, magical pioneers, and people who take their magic to places nobody else dares go. Dragons are known for their powerful force of will and their natural charisma and leadership, inspiring confidence in others to follow them to the ends of the earth.

Dragon House is known for having the most graduates that go on to positions of top leadership in the Academy. It’s also known for having the most graduates that go on to become hexologists, but they don’t like to talk about that one quite as much.

##### The House

Dragon House stands right at the crest of Jewelstone Hollow, a place where the ground itself is rich with magics, owing to the massive arcane crystalline structures that run deep under the surface. The House is imposing, in a word: tall and dark, black stone with flying buttresses, turrets, great banded doors and stained glass windows depicting scenes of Starfall’s founding and of the Great Dragon’s life. Alongside the cemeteries and dark copses of gravewood trees that fill in the path winding through Jewelstone Hollow towards Dragon House, it’s a scenery to inspire any Gothic artist.

It’s also a scenery flush with magic—Dragon House is the House the most imbued with magic from its environs, and it shows when you walk in. Every House has its mysteries, but Dragon House especially is full of statues waiting to awaken long-dormant enchantments, wood-panel walls that split open when whispered the correct chant to reveal forgotten tunnels, and more urban legends than you could fit in the tomes that line the shelves and are, in fact, also enchanted.

##### Points of Interest

:::quote
***The Great Dragon was the first one to shapeshift.*** *It makes sense Dragon House seems like a different House from each different angle you enter.*   - **Trundale, legendary illusionist with a disputed true identity,***rumored to be a Dragon House graduate*
:::

Dragon House is intricate and sometimes maze-like in its dormitory halls, and there are many rooms, nooks and crannies, that are only known to the select few students who live close to it and explore the halls. Because of this, everyone has their own view of what the points of interest in Dragon House are.

The dorms are often connected to common rooms, not the self-contained living spaces of Scorpion House nor the grand sections of Eagle House, but small, cozy spaces where students gather spontaneously. The Great Dragon’s ethos lives on in this design: Dragons don’t need strict structure and leadership, they need to be nurtured to create their own groups, their own organizations, their own leadership.

That said, when someone says Dragon House, what comes to mind is the grand entry hallway, over stone floors that wind through different sections, up the two great curved stone stairwells, and up to the centerpiece, where the House Artifact, the Bloody Crown, stands relentless vigil on the mantle of the great fireplace.

It’s actually a fake, of course. The real one is stored in a secure location. There have been incidents of students trying to take it, and, well, that’s a bad idea.

Hollowheart Garden is a key location as well—the dark, manicured gardens behind the House that sprawl out into the scenery of Jewelstone Hollow, dressed in magic that throws the whole gardens into a perpetual state of murky twilight, to allow cultivation of rare and powerful magic plants and creatures that could normally only grow in strange magical places. The Garden is a maze in of itself, with many rumors of hidden places behind trap doors in the mausoleums scattered in plots among the gardens or of ancient trees that conceal secret passages, and errant first-years are often getting lost among the foliage.

Maybe you can be one of them!

##### Founder and Culture

The Great Dragon, Isvaldarhyxvynn, or V for short, is synonymous with leadership, with ambition, with the drive for greatness and to gather others in search of ever greater heights.

In other words, it’s main character syndrome all the way down.

:::quote
***The world needs protectors.***   - **V, the Great Dragon**
:::

The Great Dragon is the oldest of the Founders—as a true dragon, they date back to the sixteenth century, although they’re not sure of the exact date either. A remnant of the time when dragons ruled unquestioned over humankind, V bucked the wisdom of draconic dominion, fascinated by the seemingly endless adaptability of the human form to channel ley magic, and sought to increase humans’ access to ley line networks and see where their study of magic could lead them.

This was, to put it lightly, not popular with the other dragons. After a hundred years of constant conflict and assassination attempts, V eventually proved themself powerful enough to earn the respect of the dragons, claiming the title of *the Great Dragon* lauded by humans and dragons alike, one of humanity’s foremost protectors against dragons. V’s experiment worked: humans showed enough magic potential it was cause for concern, and V ended up gathering a few of the most influential early arcanists to create an academy dedicated to formalized magic study at the point where the ley lines converged the most, and Starfall Academy was born.

For better or worse.

:::quote
***Humans are so maligned for the creation of the copperbloods.*** *As if the dragons didn’t spend a thousand years killing humans before humans turned the table. But the saints be damned if the victim stands up for themselves.*   - **Calissa Redmare, magic philosopher**
:::

The humans’ creation of the copperbloods that wiped out most of the dragons was seen as a profound betrayal, and V hardened over the years since then, until they were driven to fanatical control over humans in the name of protecting them, and the other Founders worked together to seal V away in an interplanar prison. During the Basilisk Incident, the most major modern incident of Starfall’s development, V sealed away their draconic power to return to the world as a human, making them one of only two Founders to still be alive in the modern day.

That hasn’t stopped V from making themself a prominent leader in modern magic politics, which embodies the Dragon House ethos: always taking charge and positioning themselves to be leaders within their communities. Dragon House students are known for being stubborn, confident, and incredibly daring—*impossible* is a personal challenge for a Dragon.

If you’re playing a Dragon, you too are likely driven by some kind of passion. This might manifest in any number of ways: maybe you want to be the greatest arcanist in your field, or you want to head a movement to drive up institutional change, or maybe you want to uncover long-buried hexological magic and see your enemies’ heads roll. That last one is discouraged, but you wouldn’t be the first Dragon to set out down that path.

#### Dolphin House

:::quote
***Dolphin House is beautiful.*** *Every inch of it. Just don’t go downstairs. The view is much better from upstairs anyway.*   - **Professor Dahlia Quinn, illusionist**
:::

A sleek, modern building set on the banks of Glimmerdeep Lake, Dolphin House stands out from the other Houses of the Academy for its contemporary design, which is only partly an aesthetic choice: the original House was destroyed before the campus wards were established, meaning that the current House is much younger than the other Houses, minus Scorpion House.

But remnants of the old House lie buried underneath. And that’s an unintentional metaphor for Dolphins: the House of social grace and charm, Dolphins are often glimmery and polished and pretty on the surface, but they can hide deceptive layers under the surface.

As much as they’re known for their charm, their ability to command the floor at any social event, and their connections knowing a personal link to any given person in the Academy, Dolphins are also known for their cunning, delicate social manipulations, and their ability to put on a fake face for any situation.

Dolphin House is popular with illusionists, both for the natural illusion magics that are rich in Glimmerdeep Lake and for the shared ethos: to create something beautiful and flawless, like the glimmering balls and dances the House is known for, and at the same time, to lie their way out of any situation they’re in.

Most of them are just really chatty and like parties, though.

:::quote
***I guess some Dolphins are fine.***   - **Harriett Cloudless, Eagle House graduate,***who married a Dolphin*
:::

##### The House

Dolphin House is known for its white marble and ornate glass walls, and its wide-open promenades and tall halls alike are decorated with lyrevine, a magic plant with distinctive pale gold leaves that only grows around Glimmerdeep Lake.

The House is the brightest of the five, and is always alive with activity, another party or social hour happening in another one of its big halls and magic chambers every time you pass through.

But under the surface, unused doors blocked with seals reserved for the most serious academy staff open the way to the Old House, which, these days, is solidly fey territory. Students are strictly forbidden from venturing down into the Old House, and any who go for it anyway, ideally, tend to come back a little traumatized.

It’s ideal because if somebody comes back cheerfully from the Old House, they’re probably a fey changeling, and saints only know what happened to the original person.

##### Points of Interest

Dolphin House is built around big, beautiful rooms that don’t necessarily serve any purpose in of themselves. They don’t need to: putting a big room in front of Dolphins is like putting a cardboard box in front of a cat. They’re *going* to go inside and set up a party. All you have to do to make sure they have a great time is leave the room unoccupied.

The most noteworthy parts of the House are the Constellation Halls, twelve of them named after the twelve houses of the Zodiac, each one with their own character and what kinds of events are typically hosted in them. Students reference other places in the House relative to the Constellation Halls—a student might say their dorm room is directly upstairs from Capricorn Hall, or they might ask for a meeting in the corner room just off from Aries Hall, or they might say there’s a *really* cool door hidden under Virgo Hall. That last person is probably a fey changeling trying to trick somebody into entering the Old House. Ignore them.

##### Founder and Culture

:::quote
***Dolphins are so intimidating.*** *But you make friends with the right one—just one—and suddenly the world is at your fingertips. Bring a flower—they love flowers.*   - **Professor Elena Mars, counterhexologist**
:::

The Great Dolphin, Isadora, was the last of the Founders to meet the Great Dragon, and her illusive techniques laid the foundation for the transmutative magics that gave the Founders their mythical creature forms—and that eventually culminated in the creation of the copperbloods.

Isadora was a restorer long before the field of restoration was formally established, traveling across North Europe in the eighteenth century curing the incurable in exchange for influence and knowledge. When word reached her of the Great Dragon collecting great arcanists in the American colonies under the brewing conflict, she had herself invited across the Atlantic to meet the Great Dragon in person, where they had a legendary secret meeting for four days and three nights, after which the initially skeptical Great Dragon emerged naming Isadora as a proud ally of the effort.

Which turned out to be a mistake, because Isadora planted a trap in the seal where V was closed away and then murdered the other Founders in cold blood, all in a valiant effort towards immortality. She failed, and had her lingering soul energy eventually exorcised from the ley lines, wiping her extant influence from the world where even the other departed Founders still exert their will from beyond the grave, but credit where credit is due, she was doing really well for a while there. Only the Great Dragon knows what happened in that meeting of four days and three nights, and they don’t want to talk about it.

Even with Isadora having a bit of a sordid legacy in retrospect, many Dolphin House students want to embody her skill, with a “four-day gambit” being a figure of speech used by Dolphins to refer to an extended effort to convince somebody of something or insinuate themselves into a movement or organization.

:::quote
***Power is brutish only for brutes.***   - **Isadora, the Great Dolphin**
:::

The idea of exerting power without ever casting so much as a flicker is key to Dolphin culture, and plenty of socialites and masterminds hail from these halls. Dolphin House graduates are renowned as politicians, diplomats, and as operatives to create social movements.

Of course, it merits saying that Dolphin House is also very much the party house. Some people are in Dolphin House because they want to become invisible operatives who can manipulate the entire social fabric without ever touching a wand, their words building and breaking empires, while some people are in Dolphin House because they like parties. Both types are integral to the House’s culture.

If you’re playing a Dolphin, social influence is everything to you. Consider what that looks like for your character—do they want to be a charismatic figure recognized by all, a celebrity, a politician, a leader? Do they want to be a behind-the-scenes string-puller, or maybe they want to be the ultimate fixer, always with the right person for any task? Or do they just like people and want to be in all the events and make friends?

If your character is shy and quiet, there’s a precedent for that too—many people join Dolphin House specifically to learn how to talk to people. Sometimes it’s for their own sake, and sometimes it’s because they’re the black-sheep child of a magic family and their parents make sure they go to Dolphin House to fix their interpersonal issues. People come to Dolphin House from all kinds of angles, but one thing is constant: you’ll have six new friends before your first week is out, and then they just keep snowballing from there.

That said, every House has its own specific relations towards each other House, often rooted in how the Founders felt about one another, legacies that have carried on in the culture.

#### House Teams and House Games

:::quote
***A House team’s sworn duty is to get up to mischief.*** *Its official duty is to play games and support camaraderie with their fellow House members. Starfallers make excellent multitaskers!*   - **Clarice Hamilton, administrator for the House Games Commission**
:::

An integral part of rivalry, of course, is competition. House games are a varied set of competitions put on by Starfall administrators, structured contests where people form House teams and participate, vying for the top spot to bring glory to their House and to their team.

There are as many different types of games as there are participants, and not even the most dedicated team takes part in all of them—roughly the Starfall equivalent of sporting events, a given team will generally form around a few different types of game. Some, such as the **Witch’s Run,** are famous the world over and adopted by other magic academies, while others are smaller but still mainstays that are run a few times per semester, and others still are seasonal or one-off games.

Because of the variety-game nature of the House games, many House teams might specialize in a few specific games that are more regular but will crosstrain to compete in a variety of different games. The major exception is **Witch’s Run** teams—the most widespread and famous of the House games, most of its teams are considered in a separate category referred to as **Runner teams** who don’t engage in other House games.

A few well-known House games that happen often include:

1. **The Witch’s Run:** A checkpoint-based race through the wild areas of Starfall. Participants race in teams up to 5 people, carrying a device called the **sigil** that cannot be teleported. Whichever team gets a member to carry a sigil through each of the checkpoints first is the winner. **Runners** train for the three key roles of the game: slowing down their rivals or knocking their sigils away, defending and supporting their mainline runner, and running like hell. The **Witch’s Run** is famous enough that its best participants become celebrities on the world stage, the game popular to follow even among non-arcanists.
2. **The Patrol Cup:** Community service dressed up as a game. Starfall’s wards are always being threatened with creatures trying to work their way through, across this and many other planes. Teams patrol the outer perimeter of Starfall along its wards and get points for finding and patching vulnerabilities and driving away creatures attempting to break through.
3. **The Planar Trip:** A planehopping challenge run to collect target materials from a collection of different planes. The game is controversial for its high injury rate, traveling to the high-risk areas of other planes, but planehoppers are the ultimate adrenaline junkies and would never consent to having the game cut or, saints have mercy, made safer.
4. **Domineer:** A four-team game centered around a massive magic construct, such as a draco golem or a metal basilisk or similar. The construct is inert, and the four teams each vie to overpower the magic of the other three and control the construct to shatter a magic core held by each team, and whoever has their magic core last is the winner. Typically the game is played in tournament format, with the two losing teams eliminated and the runner-up going to a loser’s bracket, and the importance of coming in second instead of third means the game tends towards temporary team dynamics of two teams joining to defeat the other two and then facing each other.
5. **Reliquem:** A two-part game based around artifacts, which famously takes place over 24 hours: 23 hours and 50 minutes for a team to create an artifact that will hold up for long enough to be used in a 10-minute duel at the end, where the participants are not allowed to use any of their own spells and have to duel their opponent with only their artifact. Despite the prolonged nature of the game, **reliquem** fans are perhaps the most rabid and obsessive fans of all the games and will typically stay up for 24 hours watching it just like the participants. Something about artifacts really makes the hyperfixation pop off in people.

If you’re incorporating House games into your Starfall game, you can improvise any number of competitions—the administration is always coming up with new game concepts to see what sticks. Games that lend themselves to larger or smaller teams, games based around duels, games based around specific magic fields, games based around physical achievement, there are as many games as you can think of.

:::quote
**CHARACTER PORTRAIT****Harriett Cloudless (she/her)** *Chronomancer, spell designer, philanthropist, and often cited as the greatest arcanist alive today, Harriett started out—as far as anyone knew—as one of the strange ones who participated in the Witch’s Run solo. Of course, that was just Harriett in this timeline: actually the forty-ninth incarnation of herself in a centuries-long time loop, she was the one whose team stopped the Great Dolphin Isadora from destroying the ley lines and Starfall Academy with them during the Basilisk Incident, unearthed the buried identity of the Great Scorpion Ariin, broke a century-old blood hex that was poisoning the Great Dragon V, and is credited with having “saved the world.”**Harriett Cloudless, who hates attention, is very mad about the attribution.**After the events of the Basilisk Incident, Harriett helped found Scorpion House together with the head of the Argon clan Ember Argon, the House’s first Champion, and then stepped back to become a spell designer. She hoped it would lead to a quiet life, but instead, Starfall Academy named its emblematic clock tower Cloudless Tower on her behalf—a decision on which she was not consulted—and her spell designs became so sought-after that she made vast fortunes off of it.**These days, she runs the Cloudless Foundation, a charity organization intended to help people unlock their own arcane potential to empower community leaders, but in reality, she spends her time more focusing on her magic while her wife Alessandra manages the day-to-day operations of the Foundation.**When asked if she missed not getting more time to pursue her Witch’s Run aspirations, Harriett said the following: “Who are you? Wait, was this a press conference? I’m leaving.” She then left.*
:::

The teams themselves are an important part of House cultures. Typically composed of 3-9 people, House teams are officially registered groups, each with a faculty advisor who helps them develop their skills and balance it with keeping up with their studies. The bonds people form with their teammates are for life, and they very frequently get involved in other adventures around Starfall, usually to their faculty advisor’s dismay.

They often go beyond just the team members themselves: younger teams will have unofficial mentors, while more established teams might have fans. A team that doesn’t have its own alchemist might have alchemy contacts, one without its own enchanter might have enchantment contacts, and so on, while all teams will likely have contacts to keep topped up on materials and information, especially on rival teams.

Rivalries are rampant in the House games circuit: rarely with other teams of the same House, but especially between Houses with their own rivalries. Dragon and Eagle House teams are almost always at odds, while Scorpion and Dolphin House teams put on nicer faces with each other but are, under the surface, maybe even more vicious. A given House team will often form some deep connections with other teams—a Dolphin House team might have an Eagle House team that’s their mortal enemy, a friendly rivalry with a Boar House team while the leads of each team are dating each other, and might be trading resources back and forth with another Dolphin House team.

The only rewards from House games are recognition—the Academy has always held a hardline stance on that topic, wanting the games to be for the love of the sport. Starfall Academy prioritizes the well-being of all its students, so that every student on its campus is well taken care of and arcanists can pursue glory in the House games just for the passion of it.

But it’s impossible to deny that doing well in the games gets a team and its members some very tangible benefits. Inevitably, it’s the more powerful and connected students who are at the top of the games, and ranking well gets people invitations to these powerful circles, as well as building the members’ renown and opening doors for them.

People also bet on the games. It’s illegal. But they keep doing it anyway. People will be people.

#### House Champions and the Trials

The Houses also have their internal rivalries in the spring semester during the **Championship Trials**, when the five **House Champions** are selected over a set of three trials. The **Champion** of each House is the highest honor a student can achieve at Starfall Academy, a student recognized as the highest example of a given House, representing that House in official ceremonies.

Every year, a panel of professors from each House appoints five exemplary students from that House as **Championship Nominees,** and the twenty-five selected **Nominees** end up in five teams, each with one **Nominee** from each House, representing a potential cohort of **Champions** for the upcoming year. The winners from the three trials are crowned **Champions** for one year, potentially coming back the next year to defend their title.

In general, your character won’t be participating in the **Championship Trials**, as it represents a massive apex that many students can only dream of, but no student would dream of ignoring the **Trials** when they’re happening, usually with strong feelings on which of the **Nominees** should win.

The **Nominees** tend to be exceptional students who are often campus celebrities if not celebrities outright, so it’s likely your character has heard of one or two or ten, unless they really live under a rock. They might even be friends with one.

It is perfectly normal to show up to the trials with fan merch to show support for your pick from the teams or even just for a specific **Nominee.** Don’t let your friends who are at the event with you and judging you for it crush your dreams.

Of course, if you do well enough for yourself, the GM might even end up giving you an opportunity to participate in the **Trials.** If you’re comfortable being a superstar celebrity.

Starfall Academy is a worldwide-renowned name, too, which means for every person in the stands cheering at the **Trial,** there’s a hundred more people watching on TV or just getting the highlights from Instagram while people weigh in on who’s going to win, because the moment the **Trials** start, suddenly everyone on the internet is an armchair arcane expert.

## Justice and the Enforcers

Getting into trouble is a key part of Starfall’s culture, which means inevitably the subject of the **Enforcers** and their **Enforcer’s Garrison** is an important part of the Starfall world.

Although the parallels exist, the Enforcers are not a one-to-one analogue with police. The closed-community feel of Starfall Academy, the Enforcers’ emphasis on restorative justice, and the sheer impossibility of truly getting rid of a radicalized dangerous arcanist, together mean that the Enforcers have a more people-first emphasis on community and protection than systemic corruption.

To be clear, that’s not to say the Enforcers are all good guys. Power corrupts, there are plenty of corrupt individuals in the Garrison, and as most Starfall stories involve a punk element, the Enforcers are usually at least loosely antagonistic to the heroes’ causes.

However, it isn’t a fundamentally flawed organization as many police analogues are in similar punk settings. More conflict mediators, community watchdogs, bodyguards and adjudicators of difficult disputes than they are “monopolists of violence on behalf of capital,” the Enforcers serve a complex role of guardians of right and wrong who not-infrequently get that call wrong.

This comes, if nothing else, from the fact that oftentimes, the Enforcers’ worst enemy is the Enforcers. The Garrison is frequently at odds with itself, and internal conflicts keep any one group from dominating the power landscape.

Because of the fluid nature of justice and the Trouble system, the characters in your game will likely cross paths with Enforcers in many different contexts: a Watcher who recognizes the party is in trouble and jumps to help them, the suspicious Enforcer who wants to protect the corrupt administrator and put the party in the Garrison, and the Captain who has to deal with the same person getting flung onto Garrison floor for the same crime for the third time this week.

Getting brought in by the Enforcers is almost a rite of passage for Starfallers, and in a game, is hardly the end of the story. Oftentimes, getting pulled into the Garrison for questioning or even for a hearing or punishment leads to new connections with the Garrison and with other people kept in the Garrison.

### Hierarchy

:::quote
***Call me Madame Enforcer.***   - **Baya Arclight**
:::

The Enforcers answer to the **Head Enforcer**, who rarely intervenes in ground-level cases and disputes, more often representing the Garrison in political discussions and spearheading large, complex investigations.

After the Head Enforcer, technically, is the **Deputy Enforcer,** but the role is largely one of political ceremony. At Starfall, it’s a role infamous for its fast turnover: Deputy Enforcers somehow keep attracting scandals and getting involved in danger or leaving for mysterious reasons, and the joke among the Enforcers is roughly “shouldn’t take me too long, give me two to four Deputy Enforcer terms.”

Practically speaking, below the **Head Enforcer** next are the **Lieutenant Enforcers,** each of whom preside over a specific branch of operations, such as internal investigations, logistics, Watcher affairs, intelligence-gathering, patrol and security, detective operations, and so on.

Each **Lieutenant** has a collection of **Enforcer Captains** who report to them, handling teams of ground-level operatives on a more granular topic—for example, **Captain of Security Affairs for Marveille, Astral, and Dragon’s Walk** or **Captain of Internal Artifact Logistics.**

**Junior Lieutenants** operate as a direct right-hand to a given **Lieutenant,** often representing them in meetings and diplomatic affairs as well. The same concept applies for **Junior Captains** with **Captains**, representing the primary and secondary role rather than necessarily representing seniority and power.

By contrast, Junior Enforcers and Senior Enforcers are an important component of rank among the ground-level operatives—operatives who prefer to promote horizontally than vertically, remaining in their direct operative role but taking more influence and power within the Garrison, with a whole system of seals denoting rank and influence in a framework that only makes sense to Enforcers.

And of course, **Apprentice Enforcers** are at the lowest rung: essentially student interns who show promise in their classwork and express interest in joining the Garrison, they are given low-level authority and responsibilities to prove themselves to the Garrison and potentially join the ranks of the Enforcers immediately after graduation.

Even though it’s officially the lowest rank available to the Enforcers’ Garrison, it’s a difficult position to obtain, and so **Apprentices** are often in the curious position of having more soft power and respect than some of the ground-level operatives while being lower-ranked than the guy who comes in to replace the toilet paper while the bathroom enchantments are down.

(The Garrison takes their custodial staff roles seriously. He who stocks the toilet paper stocks the nation.)

### Inside the Garrison

:::quote
***The Enforcer’s Garrison is the worst place on earth.*** *They make it comfortable. It’s insulting. Do you know who I am? I should be chained to a burning rock in Hell! Why do you have croissants on the table? Are you not taking me seriously!?*   - **Marin Endrya, infamous rascal**
:::

The Garrison takes pride in being civilized: holding cells are comfortable living arrangements, and “people in holding” (Enforcers bristle a little at them being called “inmates”) are engaged in community exercises, with communal meals from the same food supplied to the Great Hall, personal enrichment programs, access to extensive libraries and magic practice grounds, and in lighter holding conditions, are still allowed to attend classes and potentially other events with Enforcer approval, just with Watcher supervision.

The important aspect of being held in the Garrison is the loss of freedom. But Starfallers especially hate losing their freedom, so breaking someone out of the Garrison is a story as old as time.

The Garrison is a large complex, with different divisions for holding areas, for work spaces, for offices, and for the courts where both civil and criminal matters are litigated.

Hearings are often held with an eye towards holistic justice: defendants will argue how their actions were justified or helped Starfall instead of just arguing innocence, and the Enforcers attempt to take everything into context. “Matters of significance” are presided over by a spectral jury, spectral remnants of a chorus of souls that can adjudicate cases quickly and fairly, while “matters of great significance” are presided over by both a human and a spectral jury, after overreliance on the development of the spectral jury lost the Enforcers some public trust for some time.

Most Enforcers are complex, messy combinations of public servant, administrator of justice, and dealer of force. And most of them believe in the cause of the Garrison as fundamentally a good one, albeit one that has its flaws and that always needs another reform.

Rascals see them differently. But, I mean, yeah, of course they do.

## The World

:::quote
***Sometimes I wish we’d never figured out this magic thing.***   - **Kendra Bailey, Enforcer Captain**
:::

Magic extends beyond just Starfall Academy, even if Starfall is often considered the heart of it. The whole world is wrapped up in extensive magical politics, more and more with each passing year, although the majority of magic activity is concentrated around the magic academies in various places.

The system is set in the modern era—firebreathing dragons, mysterious scriptoriums and forgotten tomes exist alongside smartphones, the internet, and VR porn.

Magic, in this case, despite its ancient roots, is still something of a recent invention: many distinctions are made between traditional or indigenous magic and formalized magic study, which only really began with a strong canon of study with the founding of Starfall Academy around the time of the American Revolutionary War in the 1700s.

Before then, magic was the domain of dragons, who kept to themselves and generally only intervened in the lives of humans to stamp out any ambitions of magic among them. As with technology, the study of magic has exploded with ever-bigger leaps forward in the past hundred years, and the world is, in many cases, struggling to keep up with the rapid development of new, more powerful and far-reaching spells and artifacts.

And in a modern, interconnected world, academies all over the world vie for dominance—and world governments struggle to keep up with what magic means for them. Or to find ways to leverage that magic for their own purposes.

### Magic Academies of the World

:::quote
***Rose Sorcerers are sweethearts.*** *Still, if you see one, run for your life.*   - **Professor Thaís Santana, warder**
:::

Some of the most prominent other academies on the world stage include:

- ****Mayweather Academy of New Albion,** always referred to as just Mayweather, set on the Connecticut coastline, an institution that goes back long before Starfall, is a much loftier and exclusive academy than its nearby cousin. Part of the **tri-academy consortium** with Starfall Academy and Elara Magica Academy, the three share resources, history, and a place on the world stage. Its symbol is the Pegasus, which every student learns to ride as part of their education.**
- ****Elara Magica Academy,** situated in Ontario close to the US border, has a cozy private-school atmosphere compared to its much larger neighbor Starfall. Compared to other academies, Elara Magica has a more rounded education, with humanities, arts, and science alongside magic studies. Its magical programs aren’t lesser, though—they are especially known for artificy and enchantment. Part of the **tri-academy consortium** with Starfall and Mayweather, it’s often overlooked as the runt of the three.**
- ****Baranomichi Colégio,** in southern Brazil, is a magic multicultural academy born of Japanese roots in Brazil. Originally formed by outcasts from the Japanese school Tenchi Hikyou Gakuin, Baranomichi has the reputation as the ultimate rebels’ academy, where anything goes as long as you can back up your ambition.**
- ****Arcane Collegium of Albion,** in the Welsh countryside, generally just **Albion Arcane,** is an old institute that prioritizes the ancient art of artificy. Essentially the grandfather of the major US-based academies, including Starfall, it laid the foundations for what would become modern formalized magic study.**
- ****Volshebnikov Roza Magicheskaya Shkola,** or **Volshebnikov Roza** in general, or **Volroz** if you’re lazy and/or trendy, is the largest magic academy in Russia, along the banks of the Sea of Azov, close to the Russian city of Rostov-on-Don. Compared to the other institutions in Russia, Volshebnikov Roza has a strong international focus, and it’s famous worldwide for its Rose Sorcerers, students who spend a year studying at other magic academies in the world before returning, in the interest of promoting international exchange of new magic ideas. Fate tends to follow Rose Sorcerers—something dramatic always happens wherever they go.**
- ****Baagh Ke Panje Kee Akaadamee,** just **Baagh Ke Panje** for short, is the world’s largest magic academy by size and population, established in northern India in the 19th century as a national rallying cry against the colonialist presence. The teaching style draws on both ancient traditional forms of magic and modern formalized magic study in unison.**
- ****Yù Tù Huànshù Xuétáng,** or **Yutu Huanshu** in the West, is one of the world’s oldest magic academies, near modern-day Beijing and originally used more as an administrative center than a school of magic. Its purpose has evolved over the nearly two thousand years since its founding, but today, it is a world leader in illusion and draconology. It’s regarded as one of the world's most beautiful academies, if you can handle the stairs.**
- ****Tenchi Hikyou Gakuin** is another academy with old roots, set in Kyoto and originally serving as a center for magic and art, both practiced as one discipline by Heian nobles. These days, it has a strong international reputation, especially for its Arcane History graduates, and it runs a nontraditional teaching program where resident scholars live on campus and give freeform seminars at their leisure for students to attend as they like, instead of structured classes. The graduation exams are famously difficult enough, though, that Tenchi Hikyou graduates are prized the world over.**

You could choose to set your game at one of these other academies instead of Starfall or even to make your own, but even if you stay at Starfall, in a modern interconnected world, the existence of other academies will still affect the world. Important arcane figures may be visiting from another academy, a Rose Sorcerer might transfer from Volroz into your class, or a magic conspiracy might have its roots running back to a rogue arcanist from Baranomichi.

And of course, Starfall is always trying to outperform its rivals, both on the world stage against major players like Albion Arcane and Yutu Huanshu, and also on the local stage against Mayweather and Elara Magica.

### Political Magic

The world is complicated, politics are complicated, and magic is complicated. It’s only natural the three would form an unholy trinity.

Magic academies are political flashpoints in many ways, and the eyes of the world are on the academies and their graduates. Higher-level campaigns may end up entangled in larger schemes, from political figures trying to buy magic influence, to outright conspiracies.

And nobody is quite as effective at controlling the politics of magic as the **United States Federal Department of Magic**. For better or worse. (Probably worse.)

The Federal Department of Magic, or the **FDM,** is a branch of the United States government that handles intelligence and operations on the magic front. They essentially style themselves as the Enforcers on the world stage, although with a lot more meddling—an FDM agent inserting themselves into the business of a foreign academy or government dealing is a tale as old as time.

:::quote
***When you’ve got a magic problem, the FDM steps in.*** *Then you’ve got two magic problems.*   - **Catherine Dawson, novelist**
:::

The bulk of their work, though, is spent tracking down and bringing to justice rogue arcanists on a higher level than any academy’s Enforcers ever could, and better equipped for the magic than any police force would ever be. FDM agents are highly skilled, impeccably trained, and often find themselves in encounters with some of the greatest—and worst—arcanists in the world.

Of course, they often cause as many problems as they solve. And many of their ranking members go on to become those worst-arcanists-in-the-world that they seek. But that doesn’t change the fact that FDM agents are seen as some of the highest echelons of magic training.

They’re also the subject of, like, a million cheesy thriller books and movies. Arcanists typically can’t stand them. Too many inaccuracies about magic.

That said, although the US has the morally-dubious-secret-agency thing down to an art, there are plenty of other players in the field. The **Red Deer Guard** from Germany’s **Schwarzwald Akademie** serves a similar role in Europe, while the Asian and South American fields are more contentious, many smaller organizations often butting heads, and Africa’s magic scene is often more patrolled by political agents from other regions.

And Australia does their own thing. Mostly they just have to worry about the spiders. You know how it is.

### Leaving the Academy

Although the games revolve around the academies, there’s nothing saying you have to spend all your time on campus. Sometimes you might go out on a quest to find something or someone, or you might even leave behind the academy altogether. (It’ll probably call you back in the end. It always does.)

Leaving magic academy grounds is often a bit of a power fantasy—surrounded by nothing but other arcanists at the academy, it’s easy for an arcanist to forget that their powers are exceptional until they go out into the larger world and find the biggest fear most people have is getting hit by a car, which, like, sure, it *hurts,* but it’s not going to stop your day if you’ve got stuff going on, you know?

There are a couple of drawbacks to this level of power, though: one is that nonmagical people tend to fear and distrust arcanists like capricious demigods, and another is that other arcanists are also out there taking advantage of that power. When your path leads you outside of the academy, it’s often leading to another arcanist, which can result in something like a superhero battle if it should come to blows, with everybody around the arcanists having no choice but to run and hide.

:::quote
***It’s easy pickings for an arcanist,*** *going out into the world and leveraging that power to gain endless money, power, adoration. Which raises the question of why they aren’t all doing it? I think the answer is simple: they get bored. It’s no fun without a challenge, right? Who enjoys beating an ant?*   - **Head Enforcer Florence Walker**
:::

Of course, it goes without saying that your academy probably won’t be happy with you if you go and torment civilians with your magic and reflect badly on them. Going down this path puts you into the category of **rogue arcanist,** anybody leveraging magic against internationally agreed-upon standards, which will make you a target for other arcanists to track down and bring to justice.

Just because you’re superhuman, though, doesn’t mean everything will be easy. If you leave on a mission to infiltrate a political organization and stop somebody from performing a clandestine assassination there, you won’t be able to just stroll inside with a well-placed **Shatter** against anyone who tries to stop you.

You’ll also be fascinating to many people, unless you hide your magic. Some people try to learn magic by their own devices, and they’ll likely want to connect with you in hopes it’ll lead them back to the Whisperkeeps or something. Maybe you can let them down easy. Or maybe you can string them along.

## Cultures and Subcultures

Starfall Academy is enough of an institution that *Starfall culture* is a term known all across the US, over much of the world, and sometimes used as an insult on the internet when anti-arcanists spam Instagram pages about magic with hate comments about *the ideological capture of Starfall culture in modern society.* However, in reality, Starfall is big enough that what *Starfall culture* actually means depends on who you ask.

Oftentimes, Starfall culture is ambitious and driven by a pursuit for greatness, arcanists looking to leave their marks on the world, but not in an individualistic way. The team of great arcanists who band together to protect magic, strike down threats to humanity, and innovate magic in new and incredible ways is an image critical to the culture, and with so much reliance between different fields of magic, the sentiment of *a rising tide lifts all boats* tends to prevail.

Everyone’s just competing to see who can be the biggest hero and lift all the boats the most. Rare is the student who coasts by.

But in practice, the culture breaks down into many smaller subcultures, comprising a vast and ornate patchwork of a community that’s often butting heads with itself. Many students gravitate towards a **subculture** to make connections, pursue what matters to them, and find their place in their community. There are as many **subcultures** as there are groups of people, but some of the more established and well-known ones are listed below.

Your character might belong to one completely, or they might dabble in a few, or they might not really be in any **subcultures.** However, even if they don’t, consider if any of them mean anything to your character—maybe you play a **Mastermind** who knows a few good **Finders,** or you’re an **Heir** who’s often sung about by **Spectral Rockers** (and possibly hates it).

### Runners

**Runners** participate in the ubiquitous sport of the **Witch’s Run,** the star athletes who are all vying for the spotlight. These people are known to never stop moving—they wake up at the crack of dawn to get practicing, training mind, body and magic, and they take “impossible” as a personal challenge.

**Runners** run in teams, and they’re often ride-or-die with their teammates. Between the three main types of position on a Run team, there are further breakdowns of the typical personality types that emerge: offensive players are relentless go-getters who don’t acknowledge limits to anything, defensive players are loyal and dedicated friends to those they care about and always have a way to help, and the mainlines are stars who soak up attention and can handle any social situation with grace, naturals on camera.

If your character is a **runner,** there’s probably a reason they’re drawn to big, bombastic shows and raw head-to-head magic power. Any other **runners** you might have on your team are likely your closest friends and allies, while **runners** on other teams are friendly rivals or mortal enemies.

The **Witch’s Run** is spectacle as much as it is sport, and its best runners are superstars, some of the best-known celebrities in the magic world. Even if your character isn’t the mainline who really steals the show, there’s probably a reason your character is involved in such a showy sport.

**Personalities:** Explosive, go-getters, desperate for attention

**Common practices:** Sparring with friends and rivals, practice runs, attending runner parties and events to try posturing and being the coolest and most interesting person in the room

**Strengths:** The bonds with their team, powerful magic, athletic ability, natural charisma

**Weaknesses:** Drama within a Run team can quickly spiral, and also literally anything subtle

**Style:** The tracksuit with the Starfall crest and a special crest designed for the Run team is emblematic of **Runners,** and handily carries six potions and slots for four wands. Since runs frequently take place through dense woods and other areas where loose clothing could snag, the tracksuit is always fitted to the wearer, and this informs how **Runners** typically dress even outside of their running outfits, with neatly fitted clothes and any larger items, like coats or jackets, made of leather or other tough materials that don’t snag.

**Regular haunts:** **Runners** are comfortable in the outdoors, and their long runs through every corner of Starfall leads them to find odd places, which means each team will often have their own obscure spot or two out in the woods or the cliffs where they hang out. All **runners** are likely to find themselves in fields, too, where they practice sigil duels and sparring matches.

**Slang:** *Sigil* is used to refer to the actual sigil, the key object in the Run, but is also a shorthand for anything critical, mission-important, such as a person they need to win over for their cause or the target of a mission or heist. Similarly, to “hold the sigil” means to keep your focus on what’s most important. *Storming*, in allusion to most **runners**’ favorite spell **Stormstep**, means to go all out on something, whether that’s sprinting in a run, staying up all night studying for a test, or winning a person over by showering them with flattery and attention. The idea of storming is key to the **runner’s** ethos, and “keep storming” is used as a refrain and a goodbye as a signal to that ethos.

### Duelists

**Duelists** love to duel. There’s no surprises with these students: they always refine and practice magic that can help them in a duel, and they try to collect duel tokens from as many people as possible, challenging people all across campus to duels.

That said, the **duelist circuit,** as they often refer to their own culture and community, is one defined by friendly rivalries and not animosity. **Duelist** culture promotes good sportspersonship above all else, and being a sore loser gets you branded by the community as a *ghoul*—their term for a negative presence in a community, evoking the necromantic creature that thrives on spite and spreads decay. The emphasis is on being grateful that the other person gave you the opportunity to learn something new about your magic and your weaknesses.

Every **duelist** is vying to be at the top of the circuit, but it’s not a jealous scene—if anything, they can tend towards hero worship, idolizing the best **duelists** who have legendary reputations in the circuit. Still, above all else, the heart of the **duelist circuit** is about respect for all people and helping one another grow, and many new **duelists** are surprised by how many more experienced **duelists** take them on, help train them, and cheer for them to start collecting people’s tokens.

A **duelist’s** token is a symbol of pride, like a logo unique to each **duelist.** They often wear their tokens loudly, on clothing or even as tattoos, and wearing a famous **duelist’s** symbol is like wearing a famous athlete’s jersey, a sign of adoration. They keep the tokens they’ve collected from winning duels, which serves as an immediate record of every duel they’ve ever won. When you see two **duelists** who each have fifty of each other’s tokens, you know those two have beef. It’s probably homoerotic, too.

**Duelists** are known for having **spell-meets,** where they gather together to show off spells to one another. Because of the air of friendly competition, it’s often more about appreciating the cool new kinds of magic their buddies have and sharing tips and tricks they’ve found from their studies and practice. **Spell-meets** are also where they tend to take potions and herbalist compounds to chill out and have a good time and talk about life.

Some of the better-known **duelists** are celebrities in and of themselves, and they make big, public spectacles of their duels, people turning out in droves to watch. Many of the lesser-known **duelists** try and are disappointed when nobody shows up. Keep dreaming, buddy.

**Common practices:** Dueling, casting spells at practice ranges, developing new techniques for their next duel or spell-meet

**Strengths:** Powerful casting magic, often very emotionally resilient, disarming and easy to get along with

**Weaknesses:** **Duelists** tend to have a hard time taking things seriously

**Style:** **Duelists** wear all types of different clothes, but often like outfits that stand out, including bright hair colors and standout hairstyles. The most recognizable element of a **duelist’s** outfit, however, is a jacket: often leather or denim or another sturdy material, with the **duelist’s** symbol large on the back. They also wear a gazillion tokens they’ve won from other people most of the time.

**Regular haunts:** Practice ranges, spellcrafting studios, and anywhere good for a duel. With **dueling** a spectacle as much as anything else, they love dramatic backdrops for their duels and often know a few good spots that look good when they’re taking videos of their cool techniques or their latest duel.

**Slang:** *Duelbird* is a common slang term all across Starfall for someone who can’t help getting into duels, but **duelists** use *duelbird* or just *bird/birdie* as a term of endearment for one another. There’s a world of nuance: if a **duelist** says “birdie” to you, they might be cheering you on, or they might be telling you that you just said the stupidest thing in your life. It’s all in how it’s said.

### Finders

Everybody’s got a problem. That’s what **finders** are for.

**Finders** are people who take on jobs, seek out missions that people are willing to pay for, and make it happen. Typically, these jobs are dangerous. For a lot of **finders,** that’s the best part.

Some people describe **finders** as mindless mercenaries, and some people describe them as exciting heroes. Most **finders** don’t care how people describe them—although some of the best **finders** develop cult followings, particularly among **spectral rockers** who tend to run in similar circles, they’re not in it for the attention. Just the best jobs.

Well, sometimes attention. No culture is immune to the cult of personality.

**Finders** operate either solo or with **agencies,** which are not as official as the name makes them out to be—just a fancy term for a group of **finders** who take jobs together. Although **finders** tend to be less social than most of the other subcultures, gathering an exceptional **agency** is considered a rite of passage for **finders,** and soloists are typically considered not quite fully baked yet.

Although some **finders** have **agencies** with established locations and people come to them for jobs, or have networks that people send jobs through to a trusted **finder,** the typical process looks like people posting public requests for help in the underground locations **finders** are known to frequent, up on big walls known colloquially as **keepboards**—the reward paid out for a mission is referred to as its **keep.** You know, finder’s keepers? It’s clever like that. Well, they think they’re clever.

Niche spots and indie social spaces will put up a **keepboard** if they’re looking to attract **finders**, for better or worse—trouble is known to follow in their wake. Some places have a minimum **keep** for a request to go up on the **keepboard,** for high-profile spots that the best **finders** frequent. These places tend to have reputations in the broader culture. For better or worse.

**Finders** are often known for being ruthlessly competent—or at least trying to be. Their missions take on all different varieties, from helping convince somebody in a position of power of an idea, to grabbing a rare material from the demonic realm, to stealing a famous student’s wand and swapping in a fake. Most **finders** develop a reputation for a certain type of job, such as diplomacy, beast-tracking, or training. Some have a strict no-crime policy. A lot don’t.

**Common practices:** Perusing **keepboards,** attending their local place to meet their **agency** and allies, and of course, undergoing missions

**Strengths:** Diverse skillsets, an intense commitment to getting the job done, and an often unflappable attitude

**Weaknesses:** Sometimes materials are the only things that matter to **finders**

**Style:** Standing out is often impractical for **finders,** and they do a lot of their best work at night, so black tends to be a common color. A warded coat filled with tools of the trade and enchanted boots are essentials for most. Some of them go for the black leather trench coat and sunglasses, but thankfully most of them grow out of that.

**Regular haunts:** Any locale with a **keepboard** turns inevitably into a haunt for **finders,** who spend time there making connections with other **finders** and being on the lookout for new jobs—since jobs are posted publicly, getting on a competitive one an hour before other **finders** might be the deciding factor between getting the **keep** or not.

**Slang:** A place with a **keepboard** is often referred to synecdochically as a **keepboard** itself, so **finders** will talk plenty about one **keepboard** versus another. The **keep** in general is an important part of the culture: *getting the keep* for completing a mission, *earning your keep* for proving oneself as a **finder,** *playing for keeps* for taking things seriously and focusing on getting big payouts.

## Nonhuman Characters (Optional Section)

Starfall Academy is not a setting where many different types of magical creatures converge—it’s very much a humans’ world. The only other creatures with human intelligence are fey, who don’t do well with institutions; dragons, who have contentious relationships with humankind; extraplanar creatures, who often don’t gel well with things like “you have to come to class every day” or “all your classmates are made of matter;” and demons, who… well, who’s inviting a demon?

Because of all this, nonhuman characters are vanishingly rare, and those who do exist are almost always keeping their true identity a secret. However, they do exist, and if your GM agrees to let you, you may even decide to try playing one. It’s not a decision taken lightly—typically you’ll be more like an infiltrator, hiding your identity as best you can.

But everyone at Starfall is weird already anyway, so you can get away with it, if you want to give it a go.

There are several options, each with their own characteristics, both style-wise and mechanics-wise. If you choose to play one, you can adopt as much of that as you like: each option has its own mechanical effect, but you can choose to ignore it and play with standard human mechanics if you prefer. Similarly, you can be as subtle or overt with how your identity comes through in who you are as you like.

For example, you may want to play a draconic character who leans into the haughty aspects that mark most of dragonkind, but not to adopt any of the physical traits or the gameplay trait. Or you might want to play a fey character who hides their fey identity as much as possible, ignoring the personality and physical aspects of feydom but using the gameplay traits.

Whatever you want to try, work it out with the GM. You could even be open with your identity, but it will profoundly impact how you fit into the story. It is only mostly unheard of: famous spell designer Leona Belham is fey and cast out from her fey clan for too many close associations with humans and these days enjoys hosting house parties and leaving slightly unhinged Letterboxd reviews, while the Great Dragon, V, who helped found Starfall Academy, readily threw away their draconic power to protect Starfall and now lives as a human.

Most of the time, though? You kind of keep it on the down-low.

### Fey

1. **The carefree pranksters of the natural world, originators of most illusion magic and of many hexes.**
2. ****What you might be:** You may be a changeling, a fey entity who’s been snuck into human society—though these days the fey have stopped kidnapping babies in the process of inserting a changeling, since they noticed it was giving them a bad reputation. You may also be rejected by fey society or perhaps just venturing out beyond your society, often behind their backs. Unless you are a changeling, your human form is likely just one shapeshifter form you possess, but as the fey are relentless shapeshifters, it’s no less the “real you” than any of your other forms (although you may decide your character feels it is more or less representative of who they are than other forms).**
3. ****Personality traits:** Fey tend to be deeply curious about everything, sometimes too curious. They’re also well known for being tricksters, deceptive and glib, and for having an often incomprehensible moral compass, but they generally try to do right by everyone. They just have a different idea of what that looks like.**
4. ****Physical traits:** Fey are often skilled glamor artists, meaning they can look like anything. That said, between what they most easily can conjure and what they tend to lean towards, they often have more feline traits—lithe, lean, with sharp eyes and a particular grace to how they hold themselves.**
5. ****Gameplay traits:** Illusion and one other Subject of your choice rolls with Charm instead. You must **Resist** if you ever pass through a heavily warded area or must interact directly with wards.**
6. ****How people will see you:** Fey are one of the better understood of nonhuman creatures, often seen more as a curiosity, in the same way fey see humans as a curiosity. People won’t often see you directly as a threat, but they will often expect you to be duplicitous and manipulative.**
7. ****Other traits:** Fey hate oranges, famously enough that in olden days people used to make each other eat oranges to prove they weren’t fey changelings. Even the most dedicated fey trying to pass as human can’t help cringing at orange juice.**

### Draconic

1. **The one-time undisputed rulers of magic, now toppled from their thrones and forced to live alongside humans, their power is still fearsome, but dragonkind is, as a whole, humbled in the modern era. Some of them resent that.**
2. ****What you might be:** You are likely a full dragon, the archetypical fire-breathing flying icon of magic itself, but likely a very young one—full dragons’ powers remain quite immature for fifty years or so. In a mostly human world, full dragons almost all have a human form they can shift to and from, usually just to help them navigate things like social settings or just going into someone’s house without knocking it down, but sometimes as a way to integrate into human society without being detected as a dragon. You may also be the result of bioarcane experimentation—trying to mix human and dragon is a practice as old as bioarcane experimentation itself—placing you between the two worlds, belonging in neither.**
3. ****Personality traits:** Dragons are commanding leaders by nature. Powerful, solitary creatures who are used to ruling over everything in their domain, taking charge and assertively putting oneself out there is written into a dragon’s DNA. Some may be haughty and resentful of humankind having brought down the reign of dragons, while some, especially younger ones in the modern era, want to see humans and dragons as equals who can work together, or even apologetic for the oppression that humans and other creatures suffered under brutal draconic reign.**
4. ****Physical traits:** The human forms that dragons take tend to reflect their values of power and command, often tall and imposing. Getting exact colors right is difficult, and dragons in human forms often have slightly unusual eye, hair, or even skin colors, which your character might try to dismiss as magic effects or just hair dye. They also have stoic postures and often have to consciously remember to move naturally, as their standard posture tends to be “motionless statue.”**
5. ****Gameplay traits:** You can learn the spell Flight as a Basic spell and Avidar’s Wings as a Standard spell, both as either Evocation or Draconology spells. Evocation spells cannot force you to **Resist,** except via your own backfires. Your material-hoarding instincts can make you suffer: any time you end a day with less than 500 materials, you must **Resist.****
6. ****How people will see you:** Dragons are nothing if not divisive. If people find out your identity as a dragon, they tend to either think you personally are one of the dragons who subjugated the human race centuries ago, or that you’re the most badass thing they’ve ever seen. Everyone has an opinion on human-dragon relationships, and if people aren’t scared of you as they often are of dragons, they’ll want to tell you theirs.**
7. ****Other traits:** Dragons love to hoard. Since dragons reproduce asexually, the urge to hoard valuable magic items and materials is the closest dragon analogue to the human urge to have sex. (Although dragons in human form still have human morphology, so they can certainly enjoy sex too, it’s just not a hardwired instinct. Almost more akin to how humans feel about hoarding materials. It’s like yin and yang.) Your character will often want to collect materials for the sake of having them, and is likely to have a harder time turning down materials, whether as trades or just obvious bribes.**

### Demonic

1. **The masters of the abyssal realm, originators of evil, sowers of strife, and architects of disaster. Demons… well, they’re demons.**
2. ****What you might be:** Unless you want to play a character who is ontologically evil on every level, you are likely to be a person afflicted by demonic magic or a lesser demonic creature who has escaped the abyssal realm and wants to be a part of something nicer. Demons are known to erase minds and use people as puppets, and you may have once been a human but have had your memories and identity erased to create a demonic entity. You could also be the result of dark experimentation or contracts gone wrong, a child promised to a demon and born both demon and human.**
3. ****Personality traits:** Demons are evil. Assuming you are not playing an evil demon who wants to bring about the downfall of humankind, this likely manifests for you in that you’re accustomed to demons and assume evil, to some extent, from everyone around you, leading to the demonic character being small, quiet, and trying to avoid entanglements with people. Demons operate in cold logic, and even the smallest amount of demonic influence in your nature is likely to render you cool, detached, and reasoning.**
4. ****Physical traits:** Unlike many other nonhuman characters, this humanoid form probably is your true form, not a shapeshift or a glamor. You might look like anything, but you will likely have some sort of small demonic elements about your body that you hide, such as horns, spines on your back, blood-pact symbols on your body, or even more noticeable ones, such as cloven hoof-like feet, a tail, or a third eye.**
5. ****Gameplay traits:** You are immune to demonic persuasion or attempts to influence your emotions. You know the spell **Demon’s Insight** automatically, and the demon connected gives you special attention, often communicating with you unprompted. This demon may be a **True Demon.** If you spend a day without doing at least one thing the demon asks, including situations where it isn’t necessarily harmful such as following insights from **Demon’s Insight,** you must **Resist.** On any day you perform a **Downtime** action, this condition is fulfilled, as it is assumed you communicate with the demon during your **Downtime.****
6. ****How people will see you:** This is likely the one you want least to tell people about. When somebody finds out you are demonic, they are very likely to fear and distrust you, and will stay away from you as much as possible. The hardest part is that you can’t even blame them—other demons are out there approaching people in these ways to manipulate them all the time, and you can’t really prove that you’re not a demon in the ontological sense. A life as a demonic character is likely a lonely one, and if someone finds out your identity and decides to trust you anyway, it will be hard not to cling to them.**
7. ****Other traits:** The abyssal realm is hot—not a pit of fire like the depictions, but hot enough that, being demonic in the material realm, you’re probably always cold. Demonic characters will wear layers on layers and layers, which has the added benefit of hiding any demonic traits they might have. It also has the benefit of making you quite stylish.**

### Lycanthropic

1. **Those who have been afflicted with lycanthropic transformation or, in some cases, simply born one. Typically these shifters have uncontrolled transformations into a form in line with a beast, often a wolf, but some of them, especially the ones who are born lycanthropes, have more control over their form and when they shift and shift back.**
2. ****What you might be:** Your main choice is whether you’re a born lycanthrope, often raised by other lycanthropes away from human society, or if you’ve been turned. If you’ve been born a lycanthrope, your parents are both lycanthropes as well, and because of the social stigma, you’ve likely had little contact with larger human society. If you’ve been turned, you’re likely closer to an ordinary human in many ways, but you’re likely to have a difficult relationship with your lycanthropy—those who are born with it often see it as a part of themselves, while those who have been turned later often see it as something done to them. Born lycanthropes are rare even among lycanthropes in general. But maybe you’re extra special.**
3. ****Personality traits:** Since lycanthropy is an effect added onto being human, rather than a true alternate creature type, lycanthropes are typically very human in nature. However, the feral nature of the beast form manifests itself for even the most controlled lycanthropes, with a constant supply of energy that can be uncomfortable to have restrained, a hunter’s mindset, and of course, wanting to eat a lot of food.**
4. ****Physical traits:** Lycanthropy doesn’t necessarily change your physical form (except when you’re shifted, when it very much does), but for some, especially born lycanthropes, it tends to induce wild hair growth and a strong posture. Lycanthropes will often keep their hair long, mostly because it’s a pain to get it cut every week when it’s worn short. To say nothing of having to trim their rock-solid fingernails twice a week.**
5. ****Gameplay traits:** You can choose to have any transfiguratory spell cast on you fail. Choose at least one condition that induces your lycanthropy, such as the full moon, the smell of blood, a natural prey target, or, hey, maybe if you’re low on magnesium, really anything you like. When those conditions come about, you must roll **Willpower, DC18,** to avoid shifting, automatically failing on a critical failure. When you shift, you take a +6 bonus to **Body** checks, but you cannot cast magic until you shift back, after 1 hour and once the shifting condition is gone. On higher degrees of failure on the **Willpower** check, you may temporarily lose control to the feral impulses. You can also make a **DC22 Willpower** check to shift on command, but on a critical failure, you may lose control to the feral impulses.**
6. ****How people will see you:** People often both fear and pity lycanthropes—knowing that they did not ask for it and often don’t like it, they don’t want to hold anything against them, but even born lycanthropes who have been living with their condition all their lives will sometimes lose control and hurt the people around them, so people who find out you are a lycanthrope may keep some distance. Some people think werewolves are hot, though. Sometimes those people are worse.**
7. ****Other traits:** Lycanthropes often have a very keen sense of smell, which can sometimes be for the worse, especially if they’re trying to keep their identity under wraps and there’s a bad smell that’s too weak for regular humans to pick up on. They often have behavior and urges in line with their lycanthropic form, so a wolf shifter might be obsessed with swimming while a cat shifter inexplicably wants to climb onto every high object.**
